# Wallets

A **Wallet** is a balance the customer owns. It is multi-asset, and **Rehive
mirrors it on its ledger**: we record the balance, the underlying value is held
in custody. Rehive is the record, never the custodian.

A Wallet flexes on three axes, which is why it works for everything from a
consumer wallet to an enterprise running its own currency.

## Any asset

Use **stablecoins** through our providers, or run your **own currency** (local
fiat, points or tokens) on the multi-asset ledger.

## Any custody

Underneath every Wallet sits custody. The Wallet is the mirrored balance. The
custody layer is where the value is actually held.

| Custody | Who holds value | Powered by |
|---|---|---|
| **Custodial** | Held for your users, USDC under their license | Bridge |
| **Self-custody** | The end user holds their keys | Privy |
| **Your own** | You hold the value; Rehive is the record | your own treasury |

> "Self-custody" (the user's keys) is distinct from "self-operated"
> infrastructure. We avoid the term "self-hosted custody" because, in regulatory
> language, a *self-hosted wallet* already means a user-controlled one.

## Ledger optional

- **Held**: track balances and transactions on the ledger.
- **Endpoints-only (passthrough)**: provision [payment](/money/payments) endpoints
  (virtual accounts, addresses) with **no balance held**. Money lands and you
  sweep or forward it.

```sh
  -d asset=USDC -d ledger=off   # endpoints-only passthrough
```

## Standalone

A Wallet is a standalone-capable product. A self-custody wallet with no
[Payments](/money/payments) and no [Identity](/identity) is a valid bare
product: just a balance the customer owns, held in their own custody.
